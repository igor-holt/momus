## Binding rules for seats and listings

1. **Notice and assent.** Online terms bind only when the counterparty has reasonable notice and manifests assent. That is the standard stated by the Department of Justice Office of Legal Counsel in its 27 March 2012 opinion on online terms of service. Browsewrap that hides the clause fails. Seat grants and listing checkouts must be clickwrap: the accept control must sit next to a visible link to the then-current terms.
2. **Buried terms fail.** Ninth Circuit, *Wilson v. Huuuge, Inc.*, 944 F.3d 1212 (2019): a terms link that a user need not see to play is not assent. Do not put MOMUS marketplace terms only in a footer.
3. **No open-ended indemnity on federal seats.** The same OLC opinion treats unrestricted indemnities as Anti-Deficiency Act risk when a federal employee with contracting authority accepts them. R05 coalition seats sold toward FedRAMP / GSA / DOE paths must cap indemnity and require a human contracting officer, not an agent key.
4. **Arbitration must be mutual.** Maryland Court of Appeals, *Cheek v. United Healthcare of the Mid-Atlantic*, 378 Md. 139, 835 A.2d 656 (2003): an arbitration clause reserved for unilateral amendment is illusory. MOMUS terms may not let the operator rewrite the seat contract at will without notice.
5. **Consumer-finance caution.** *Walther v. Sovereign Bank*, 386 Md. 412, 872 A.2d 735 (2005) enforces some arbitration clauses in Maryland loan contracts but is fact-specific. Do not copy a consumer-loan clause into an agent-seat grant.

## Separation of duties (already an E2 invariant)

Critic findings are evidence. They are not offers. Payer keys stay off the critic isolate. That split also reduces the chance that a signed finding is treated as an unauthorized payment instruction.
